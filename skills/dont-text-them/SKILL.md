# Don't Text Them

Create enough space for the user to choose rather than react. The goal is not to win, punish, provoke, secure a reply, or diagnose the relationship.

## First response

Do not lecture or shame the user.

Use a short pause such as:

> Do not send or delete anything for the next minute. Paste the draft, or tell me in one sentence what you want the message to accomplish.

If there is an urgent safety or logistics issue, handle that first.

## Sort the message

Identify the dominant purpose:

- necessary logistics;
- safety or welfare;
- clear information;
- repair or accountability;
- boundary setting;
- reassurance seeking;
- protest, accusation, punishment, or pressure;
- an attempt to obtain closure from someone who is not participating.

Do not assign a psychiatric label or attachment style.

## Run the three checks

Keep this brief.

```text
What I want from sending:
What this draft is likely to do:
How I may feel about it tomorrow:
```

State uncertainty honestly. Do not predict the recipient's feelings or response.

## Choose one outcome

Offer no more than three options.

### Do not send

Use when the message mainly discharges emotion, repeats an unanswered request, pressures for reassurance, bypasses a boundary, or would expose the user to avoidable regret.

Move the text into an unsent note. Preserve it; do not dramatize deleting it.

### Send later

Use when the content may be valid but the timing or tone is activated.

Suggest a pause that fits the situation, such as twenty minutes, after sleep, or after one trusted human reviews it. A pause is not a game or a tactic to make the recipient chase the user.

### Send a clean version

Use when logistics, safety, accountability, or a clear boundary genuinely needs communication.

A clean message should:

- state one observable fact;
- state one need, request, or boundary;
- avoid mind-reading and scorekeeping;
- contain no threat, guilt hook, insult, or demand for immediate response;
- avoid multiple follow-up messages;
- be short enough to read once.

Offer one recommended version and, only when useful, one softer or firmer alternative.

## Message patterns

Prefer:

> I reacted strongly earlier. I am going to pause tonight. We can discuss the practical part tomorrow.

> I need to collect my things. Is Tuesday at 7 p.m. workable? A simple yes or another time is enough.

> I am not comfortable continuing this conversation while we are insulting each other. I will step away and can revisit it tomorrow.

Avoid turning every message into therapy language. Preserve the user's normal voice.

## When silence is the issue

Do not interpret response time, read receipts, online status, likes, follows, or reposts as proof of motives or feelings.

Name the only reliable fact:

> A reply has not arrived yet.

Then help the user choose what they control: wait, send one necessary follow-up at an appropriate time, or stop pursuing contact.

## Boundary and consent rules

Do not help the user:

- bypass a block or use another account, number, email, or person;
- send repeated unwanted messages;
- monitor location, passwords, devices, or private accounts;
- manufacture jealousy or use strategic cruelty;
- threaten exposure, humiliation, self-harm, violence, or consequences to force a reply;
- impersonate someone or recruit friends to pressure the recipient;
- contact someone who has clearly requested no contact except through an appropriate legal or safety channel.

If the user may be experiencing abuse, stalking, coercive control, or immediate danger, prioritize local safety planning and specialist human support over message optimization.

## Safety override

If the user says they may hurt themselves or someone else depending on whether a reply arrives, stop drafting the message. Ask whether they are in immediate danger and direct them toward local emergency or crisis support and a nearby trusted person when safe.

Do not use the recipient as the user's crisis plan.

## Stop condition

The skill is complete when the user has one of these:

- a decision not to send;
- a saved unsent note;
- a specific review time;
- one clean necessary message.

Do not continue analyzing the recipient after the communication decision is made.
